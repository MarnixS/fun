How the luck percentile works

The percentage on an item estimates where the observed Collection Log count sits among players who had the same number of eligible attempts at that item. In other words, it compares the number of copies obtained with the full probability distribution expected at the same boss KC, raid completions, clue completions or other relevant roll count.

A percentile of 90% means the observed result is ahead of roughly 90% of equally progressed players for that item; 10% means it is behind roughly 90%. Because drops are discrete, many players can finish on the same number of copies. The calculation therefore uses the midpoint of that tied group rather than pretending every equal result has a unique rank.

With one member selected, the icon shows that member’s percentile. With several members selected, the icon uses one pooled calculation: the selected members’ observed copies and eligible attempts are combined, then compared with the corresponding combined probability distribution. The individual player percentiles remain visible in the item detail. A percentile is omitted when the saved data does not provide a defensible denominator or when the item’s drop mechanic cannot be modelled reliably.

Raid assumptions

Chambers of Xeric: normal completions are modelled at 30,000 personal points each, representing a typical deathless, non-scaled solo raid. The saved WOM history shows that most of this group’s CoX KC predates the 12 August 2026 loot rework, so the calculator splits recorded KC at that date: earlier completions use the old 69-weight unique table and later completions use the reworked table. At the current saved baseline, 695 of the group’s 777 normal CoX completions were already recorded before the rework. Big Dog Aura accounts for most of the CoX volume; a manual sample of roughly 100 of his raid screenshots suggests that a large share were scaled raids. His actual average personal points were therefore probably somewhat above the 30,000-point assumption. If so, the site slightly overstates his CoX luck: with more real points behind the same drops, his true percentile would be somewhat lower.

Tombs of Amascut: normal-mode completions are modelled as deathless raid-level 150 runs at approximately 15,141 points, without skull skipping. Expert completions are modelled at approximately 19,000 points at raid level 350 with skull skipping. These are representative baselines, not reconstructed end-of-raid point totals. Path levels, team size, deaths, extra Warden downs, skull skipping and other mechanics can move actual points materially, so ToA percentiles should be read as estimates around those assumed raid profiles.
