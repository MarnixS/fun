Exactly how the luck calculation works

Item percentile first. Each calculable item starts with the observed number of copies and the estimated number of eligible rolls. The model asks where that observed count sits in the probability distribution for the same number of rolls. The displayed percentile uses the midpoint of a tied discrete outcome: probability of fewer copies + half the probability of exactly that many copies. Around 50% is ordinary; a high percentile is lucky and a low percentile is dry.

Meaningful RNG weighting. Each item percentile is converted to a standard-normal deviation (σ), then capped at ±3.50σ so one freak event cannot determine an entire account. Each item gets a gameplay-impact weight. Major progression weapons and account-defining upgrades sit at the top; normal PvM equipment is in the middle; pets, jars, cosmetics and especially clue cosmetics are heavily down-weighted. Noxious halberd components are explicitly treated as meaningful progression pieces rather than as cheap untradeable items.

Combined RNG. The headline still combines capped item-level deviations with gameplay-impact weights, but raids now also receive a pooled portfolio signal. For CoX, ToA and ToB, the model compares total valuable uniques obtained versus total expected and separately checks whether the obtained uniques skew toward higher-impact drops. The selected players are pooled before this is calculated, so one member being dry on one raid item can be compensated by another member being lucky elsewhere in the same raid. Broadly being a little dry across many raid items still counts against the portfolio, so one ordinary spoon cannot erase widespread bad luck. A transformative drop such as a Tbow, Shadow or Scythe can offset much more because it has the highest item-impact weight.

GE value. Price is not the main importance model. For gameplay-relevant items, live GE value can move the gameplay-impact weight by at most ±10%. The major exception is conceptual rather than linear: genuinely transformative raid weapons are deliberately assigned the highest base impact before the GE modifier, so a Tbow, Shadow or Scythe can outweigh several ordinary dry outcomes without raw GP value dictating everything.

Final 1–10 score. The combined meaningful RNG deviation is mapped onto a bounded scale where 5.0 is ordinary, values below 5 are unlucky and values above 5 are lucky. 1.0 and 10.0 are theoretical extremes; increasingly extreme RNG approaches those endpoints non-linearly.

Collection Log caps. A Collection Log counter can stop increasing even while more copies are obtained. The cap is item-specific: many ordinary counters stop at 250, while some stackable counters stop at 65,535. If any selected member is sitting exactly on a known ceiling, the true count is censored, so the item is excluded completely and moved to Impossible to tell. The current saved group data contains 12 unique capped entries across those two ceilings.

Nex. Nex items are excluded. Personal unique chance depends on historical contribution and MVP bonus. Much of this group's Nex KC was completed in masses/teams, and KC alone cannot reconstruct those personal shares.

Bolt racks. Bolt racks are excluded because Barrows reward potential determines whether they are on the reward table and their quantity. WOM only gives Barrows chest count, not reward potential per chest.

Chambers of Xeric. Almost all recorded CoX KC predates the 12 August 2026 loot rework, so pre-rework completions use the historical 69-weight unique table. Normal CoX assumes 30,000 personal points per completion, representative of a deathless, non-scaled solo. Timed deathless solo CM uses 62,000 points. Where saved WOM history straddles the rework, KC is split between historical and reworked tables.

Big Dog Aura CoX caveat. A large proportion, probably the majority, of his normal CoX KC was completed in scaled raids. A roughly 100-screenshot sample indicates his real average personal points were above the 30,000-point baseline. The model therefore likely makes his CoX luck look slightly better than it really was.

Normal ToA. Fixed representative model: approximately 150 invocation, deathless, without skull skipping, using a ~1/45 purple chance, equivalent to roughly 16,667 reward points.

Expert ToA. Fixed representative model: approximately 350 invocation, deathless, with skull skipping, using a ~1/16 purple chance, equivalent to roughly 21,875 reward points.
