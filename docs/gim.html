Probability-based luck analysis for any additive selection of group members, ending in one 1–10 luck score. Drop probability determines how unusual the RNG was; gameplay impact determines how much that RNG matters. Progression weapons and major upgrades carry far more weight than clue cosmetics, pets and vanity drops. GE value is only a small secondary modifier.
Exactly how the luck calculation works

Item percentile first. Each calculable item starts with the observed number of copies and the estimated number of eligible rolls. The model asks where that observed count sits in the probability distribution for the same number of rolls. The displayed percentile uses the midpoint of a tied discrete outcome: probability of fewer copies + half the probability of exactly that many copies. Around 50% is ordinary; a high percentile is lucky and a low percentile is dry.

Community progression value. Each item percentile is converted to a standard-normal deviation (σ), then capped at ±3.50σ. The overall-impact view does not treat GP value as importance. It uses a curated progression hierarchy intended to reflect how Ironman/GIM players generally value upgrades: account-defining megarares and progression-opening weapons sit at the top; broad utility upgrades such as Rigour, Fang and Lightbearer remain important even when their GE price is much lower; incremental equipment sits below them; pets, jars and clue cosmetics are heavily down-weighted.

GIM portfolio value. The same drop can matter differently depending on what the five-man group already owns. Early useful copies receive more weight than redundant copies. Shareable equipment is treated as group access rather than five isolated bank tabs. Permanent consumable unlocks such as Dexterous prayer scrolls retain strong value through the first five useful copies because they can unlock different members. Component drops receive extra value when the group collectively completes a usable item such as the Noxious halberd or Soulreaper axe.

Complementarity and compensated dryness. Complementary drops can increase each other's progression value, with a strict cap so synergy cannot manufacture unlimited score. Examples include Tbow plus group Rigour access, Shadow plus high-end magic-damage gear, Bowfa access plus enough crystal armour seeds, and Lightbearer plus strong special-attack weapons. The reverse also matters: a player can be statistically dry on an item in the raw view while the practical penalty in the overall score is much smaller if teammates have already solved that upgrade for the GIM. For example, a player with no Dex drops is still personally dry on Dex, but that dryness should not outweigh a Tbow spoon if the group already has enough Dexterous scrolls to provide Rigour access. Because the Collection Log does not record who actually consumed each scroll, this is modelled as group unlock capacity, not as proof that a specific account has unlocked the prayer.

Drop-table saturation. Items sharing the same imported source are treated as one bounded family of evidence. The combined L2 weight of a source family is capped, with especially low caps for clue tables and Barrows. This prevents content with many Collection Log slots from gaining more statistical power merely because Jagex split its rewards into more entries.

Correlated imported entries. Some Collection Log entries are multiple components or representations of the same underlying RNG event. Those entries remain visible individually in the lucky/dry lists, but the headline score collapses supported dependent sets into one shared roll group. This prevents one underlying drop mechanic from receiving several independent votes merely because Temple imports several component counters.

Combined RNG. The headline combines capped item-level deviations with portfolio-aware progression weights, while CoX, ToA and ToB also receive a pooled raid signal. Raid items retain only part of their item-level weight when they are already represented in that pooled signal; iconic megarares retain the largest residual because obtaining a Tbow, Shadow or Scythe is itself a defining RNG event. Each raid portfolio measures volume (total uniques versus expected), quality (whether the drops skew toward more consequential uniques), and coverage (whether useful different drops were collectively obtained). This lets complementary drops across members compensate for each other without allowing one ordinary spoon to erase broad dryness.

GE value. Price is deliberately secondary in the overall score. For gameplay-relevant items, live GE value can move the progression weight by only about ±6%. The separate GE impact lens is where price matters directly. This prevents cheap but account-changing upgrades from being underrated and prevents expensive incremental armour from automatically outranking transformative progression drops.

Model confidence. Imported KC is not equally informative for every mechanic. Clean solo/direct denominators retain full weight. Mechanics reconstructed from assumed historical team or contribution shares are damped, typically to 78–85% of their normal impact; distribution approximations are damped slightly. CoX and ToA are also confidence-adjusted because their historical points/raid levels are estimated rather than observed raid-by-raid. The Big Dog Aura scaled-CoX caveat receives a stronger discount. Confidence changes how strongly evidence influences the headline score; it does not change the displayed raw percentile.

Snapshot synchronization. Temple provides the item counts while WOM provides most denominators. The page checks their saved timestamps and warns if one source is materially newer. If Collection Log data is newer, very recent drops can temporarily look luckier; if WOM is newer, recent KC can temporarily look drier. The score is not silently corrected because the exact missing interim kills/drops are unknowable.

Final 1–10 score. The combined meaningful RNG deviation is mapped onto a bounded scale where 5.0 is ordinary, values below 5 are unlucky and values above 5 are lucky. 1.0 and 10.0 are theoretical extremes; increasingly extreme RNG approaches those endpoints non-linearly.

Collection Log caps. A Collection Log counter can stop increasing even while more copies are obtained. The cap is item-specific: many ordinary counters stop at 250, while some stackable counters stop at 65,535. If any selected member is sitting exactly on a known ceiling, the true count is censored, so the item is excluded completely and moved to Impossible to tell. The current saved group data contains 12 unique capped entries across those two ceilings.

Nex. Nex items are excluded. Personal unique chance depends on historical contribution and MVP bonus. Much of this group's Nex KC was completed in masses/teams, and KC alone cannot reconstruct those personal shares.

Bolt racks. Bolt racks are excluded because Barrows reward potential determines whether they are on the reward table and their quantity. WOM only gives Barrows chest count, not reward potential per chest.

Chambers of Xeric. Almost all recorded CoX KC predates the 12 August 2026 loot rework, so pre-rework completions use the historical 69-weight unique table. Normal CoX assumes 30,000 personal points per completion, representative of a deathless, non-scaled solo. Timed deathless solo CM uses 62,000 points. Where saved WOM history straddles the rework, KC is split between historical and reworked tables.

Big Dog Aura CoX caveat. A large proportion, probably the majority, of his normal CoX KC was completed in scaled raids. A roughly 100-screenshot sample indicates his real average personal points were above the 30,000-point baseline. The model therefore likely makes his CoX luck look slightly better than it really was.

Normal ToA. Fixed representative model: approximately 150 invocation, deathless, without skull skipping, using a ~1/45 purple chance, equivalent to roughly 16,667 reward points.

Expert ToA. Fixed representative model: approximately 350 invocation, deathless, with skull skipping, using a ~1/16 purple chance, equivalent to roughly 21,875 reward points.

Impossible to tell. The separate subtab contains censored counters, Nex, bolt racks, unsupported mechanics and entries where the required historical denominator is unavailable. These items contribute nothing to either overall luck index.
